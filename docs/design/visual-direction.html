Summary

What I'm proposing

Stack

Electron, React, strict TypeScript. MediaPipe Pose Landmarker in a Web Worker, with the model and WASM bundled so it works offline. Counting logic lives in a pure TypeScript package with no DOM or Electron imports, so it can be tested on recorded data.

First version (v0.1)

Milestones 1–4: first-run setup, the Lobby, a real focus timer, camera setup, explained push-up counting with correction and override, the completion moment, your game launching, calm history, dev-grade Diagnostics. Packaged for your OS.

Honesty built in

Focus time is measured; finishing the task is something you confirm. The Launch button is Ready Up's own and doesn't block your game. Counts are camera estimates you can correct, and an exercise isn't called dependable until it passes testing with you on a real camera.

Visual direction

The ready check

Every player knows the moment before a match: the lobby, the slots lighting up one by one, the countdown. Ready Up makes that moment the whole interface. The ready-check bar (three slanted slots for Focus, Move and Play) is the signature element. It's large on the Lobby and Ready screens and shrinks to a slim header everywhere else, so you always know where you are and what unlocks next.

The light is stage lighting: a dark, calm room with one lit subject. Each phase gets a single restrained tint, and the brand gold is saved for the moment you're ready.

Motion: three moments, nothing else

  • Slot fill. A slot's underline fills as its phase progresses, and the slot settles into its tint when done.
  • Rep tick. The count rolls to the next number and the depth gauge pulses once.
  • Lock-in. All three slots snap into place, READY widens from condensed to full width, and one band of light crosses the Launch button.

With reduced motion (your OS setting or an in-app toggle) each moment becomes a short fade and shows the same information. Try both on the Ready mockup below.

Override: "Can't use the camera?" asks for a reason (injury, accessibility, camera not working, other), then lets you swap the exercise, enter reps yourself, or skip Move today. Launch still unlocks (proposed). History shows "Move: self-reported (injury)" without judgment.

Phone as camera: iPhone + Mac uses Continuity Camera with nothing to install. Android + Windows 11 uses Phone Link's connected camera. Android 14+ works over USB as a webcam on any computer. Third-party apps (Camo, DroidCam, Iriun) cover the rest. Direct pairing by QR code is deferred: phones only allow camera access over HTTPS, so it needs a local certificate, WebRTC and LAN discovery.

Rep counting

What the reference does, and what we'll do instead

parhamsoltani/pushup_counter is MIT-licensed (© 2025 parhamsoltani), so reuse is allowed with the notice kept. I read its code. It's a Python script on MediaPipe's legacy API: it averages left and right elbow angles, checks one shoulder–hip–knee angle, and counts when the angle drops below 90° after being above 160°. The repo's sample video has no stated source, so we won't redistribute it.

We'll reimplement in TypeScript rather than port. The useful ideas carry over: two thresholds as hysteresis, the three-point angle, and the angle-to-percent bar, which becomes our depth gauge. We'll credit the project in third-party notices.

Issue in referenceWhat goes wrongOur approach
Counts at the bottomGo down and never push up: still countsCount on return to the top after a valid bottom
No visibility checksSide-on, the far arm is hidden and guessed. Averaging it in corrupts the anglePick the camera-facing side by visibility; require confidence
Alignment uses left side onlyWrong when your right side faces the cameraUse the chosen side
No plank checkStanding arm curls pass the check and count as push-upsTorso near horizontal, wrists below shoulders
No smoothing or timingJitter and landmark jumps can add or miss repsOne-Euro filter, minimum rep duration and interval
State survives tracking lossWalking out and back can produce surprise countsLosing tracking mid-rep voids that rep only
Silent form failuresNo way to know why a rep didn't countEvery attempt is logged as counted or rejected, with a reason
 
camera frame → Pose Landmarker (worker, local) → 33 landmarks + visibility + world coords
             → side selection (hysteresis) → One-Euro smoothing → joint angles, torso angle, framing
             → gating: in position? (named failures: not_in_plank, hips_sagging, feet_out_of_frame …)
             → rep state machine: WAITING → TOP → DESCENDING → BOTTOM → ASCENDING → TOP +1
             → RepEvent { counted | rejected, reason, minAngle, duration, side, confidence }
frames are never stored; pose recordings (landmarks only, no pixels) are opt-in from Diagnostics

Push-up defaults (to be tuned on your recordings): top ≥ 150°, bottom ≤ 100°, body line ≥ 150°, rep between 0.5 and 10 s, joint visibility ≥ 0.6. Squats: knee angle, top ≥ 160°, bottom ≤ 100° or hip at knee height. Sit-ups: torso angle from lying (≤ 20°) to up (≥ 70°); ships as Beta because pose models track lying-down bodies less reliably.

ChoiceWhyConsidered instead
ElectronOne known Chromium on every OS, so camera, WebGL and WASM behave identically. Pose tracking depends on all three.Tauri: much smaller, but uses each OS's webview, where those APIs vary. Cost of Electron: ~100 MB installer.
MediaPipe Pose LandmarkerLocal, Apache-2.0, 33 landmarks with visibility and 3D world coordinates. Model and WASM bundled; zero network.TensorFlow.js MoveNet: 17 keypoints, no feet detail, less suited to side-view floor exercises.
JSON storageSmall data, no native module to package, behind an interface so SQLite can replace it later.SQLite via better-sqlite3: needs native rebuilds per Electron version.
Hand-written state machinesTransition tables are easy to test and to display in Diagnostics.XState: capable, but more abstraction than this needs.
electron-builderNSIS (Windows), DMG (macOS), AppImage/deb (Linux), built on CI runners for your OS.Electron Forge: fine too; builder has simpler multi-target config.

Security: context isolation and sandbox on, no Node in the renderer, strict CSP with no remote origins, zod-validated IPC. No telemetry, no runtime network access, no auto-update in v1.

Verification

Synthetic tests prove logic, not accuracy

Scenario groupCasesExpected
Full repsNormal, slow, fast, pause at bottom, pause at topAll count
Partial repsHalf depth, top bounces, no lockout, down-and-stayNone count; correct reason shown
Double countsJitter at thresholds, side flip mid-rep, landmark jumps, wobble at bottomExactly one count per real rep
Not exercisingStanding arm curls, kneeling, reaching for the keyboard, walking pastZero counts
OcclusionWalk out mid-rep, hand over lens, feet out of frame, second personOnly the interrupted rep is voided; clear message
CameraUnplug, reconnect, switch mid-set, permission revokedCount kept, counting paused, then recovers
RestartQuit or kill mid-focus, mid-move, on ReadyState restored, no duplicate sessions, no lost reps

Real-camera protocol, per exercise: three sets of 10 from three positions; one mixed set of 5 full and 5 partial reps; 60 seconds of not exercising in frame; one set interrupted by walking away and unplugging the camera. I report true vs counted reps, false counts and misses.

Proposed bar for "dependable": zero counts during non-exercise movement, zero partial reps counted, at most 1 missed full rep per 30 in the ideal position.
